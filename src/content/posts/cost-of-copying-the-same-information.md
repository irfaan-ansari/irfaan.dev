An order arrives by email. Someone enters it into a sheet, then forwards the details to a colleague. Later, someone updates the customer.

Each step may take a few minutes. The interruptions can still make the day feel crowded, especially when details need correcting in several places.

## Follow one piece of information

Choose a booking, a quote request, or another item your team handles regularly. Write down where it arrives and every place it goes next. Include messages sent just to say a step is finished.

Ask the people doing the work where they check details twice or wait for a reply. The task you find most tedious may be different from the one causing them trouble.

## Try one improvement

A better form might collect details missing from every request. A shared status view could replace some update messages. Connecting existing tools might remove a copying step.

Decide how you will recognise progress: fewer corrections, less time preparing a daily list, or fewer requests sitting unnoticed.

Try the change on one process and ask the team what it helped and what still needs attention.

## Include the exceptions

The usual process may look simple until an order changes or someone enters the wrong details. Ask the team to describe a recent awkward case and how they put it right.

If information moves automatically, people still need to know where to make a correction and how to check that it reached the right places. Otherwise, you may remove a copying task while adding a new checking task.

Walk through the proposed change with the people who will use it. Have them explain what they would do when a request is incomplete or a customer changes their mind. Use those answers to refine the work before expanding it.
