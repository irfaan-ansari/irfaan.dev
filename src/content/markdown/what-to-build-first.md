title: "What should you build first when everything feels important?"
description: "Choose a manageable first version while keeping the bigger plan in view."
date: "2026-10-31"

A project list can grow quickly. You need a website, customer accounts sound useful, and someone suggests reporting before the first meeting ends.

Some ideas may be worth doing. They do not all need to be ready on the same day.

## Pick a task someone needs to finish

For a booking service, a customer needs to find an available time and request an appointment. Your team needs to receive that request and respond.

Follow the task all the way through. A polished booking page has limited use if nobody knows where requests go.

Work out what that complete task needs. Keep other ideas in a separate list so postponing them does not mean forgetting them.

## Ask why a feature matters now

What happens if it is missing at launch? Does the customer get stuck? Can your team handle a manual step for a while? Is there a business commitment involved?

Those answers help you weigh the work. A modest feature may be essential to finishing the task.

Agree on what a finished first version lets people do. After launch, use the questions and difficulties people encounter to help choose what comes next.

## Give each later idea a reason to return

A list of future features becomes easier to manage when you record why each idea matters. Beside a reporting request, note who would use the report and what decision it would help them make.

You can also record what would make the idea more urgent. Perhaps manually checking bookings is manageable at launch, but the team wants to revisit it when the work starts delaying customer replies.

Set aside time after launch to review that list with the people using the product. Ask what is taking effort now. Some early ideas may still make sense; others may be overtaken by a problem nobody could see before people started using it.
